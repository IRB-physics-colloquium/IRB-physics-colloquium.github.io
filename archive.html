Cosmology

Precision tests of fundamental physics with cosmic large-scales

Azadeh Moradinezhad · LAPTH, Annecy

The cosmic large-scale structure (LSS) provides a powerful laboratory for testing fundamental physics, including the physics of the early Universe, non-standard dark matter scenarios, and forms of dark energy beyond a cosmological constant. Current and upcoming Stage-IV spectroscopic surveys, such as DESI and Euclid, are delivering high-precision three-dimensional maps of galaxy clustering, enabling precision tests of cosmology on mildly nonlinear scales. Fully exploiting this information requires moving beyond the galaxy power spectrum to access the non-Gaussian features of the LSS, which are particularly sensitive to subtle departures from ΛCDM. In this talk, I will present recent efforts to quantify the information content of higher-order and non-standard summary statistics for constraining physics beyond ΛCDM, with particular emphasis on early-Universe signatures such as primordial non-Gaussianity and parity violation.

Quantum Physics

Physics on Quantum Computers:
Qumode-Lattices, Tensor Networks, and False Vacuum Decay

Steve Abel · Durham University, Durham

One of the most exciting prospects of quantum computing is the possibility of simulating quantum field theories beyond the reach of perturbation theory. In this talk, I will describe recent progress towards real-time simulations of non-perturbative QFT dynamics, with a particular focus on false-vacuum decay and quantum tunnelling.
These processes provide a sharp test case: they are intrinsically quantum, highly dynamical, and typically generate large amounts of entanglement, making them extremely challenging for classical simulation. I will discuss several complementary approaches to this problem, ranging from formulations designed for quantum hardware to tensor-network methods that give a structured and controllable description of entanglement. I will also comment on connections with holography, where entanglement and geometry provide a natural language for understanding non-perturbative quantum phenomena.

Mathematical Physics

From asymptotic symmetries to soft theorems and memory effects

Jan Rosseel · Ruđer Bošković Institute, Zagreb

Work by Strominger et al. has recently unveiled connections between three seemingly disparate topics, namely asymptotic symmetries, soft theorems in quantum field theory and memory effects. In this talk, I will give a pedagogical introduction to these developments. I will explain the notions of soft theorems and memory effects in the context of electromagnetism and I will explain how they can be viewed as manifestations of asymptotic symmetries, i.e., large gauge transformations that have physical effects. If time permits, I will comment on what asymptotic symmetries might teach us about potential holographic formulations of gravitational scattering in asymptotically flat spacetimes.

High-Energy Physics

The Eigenvector-Eigenvalue Identity and other pragmatic topics
in linear algebra for physicists

Stephen J. Parke · Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory, Batavia

I will discuss the mathematics and methods for diagonalizing small, but larger than 2x2 matrices, and discuss the recently rediscovered Eigenvector-Eigenvalue identity. As an explicit, pragmatic example I will use the propagation of neutrinos propagating through matter which is inherently a 3x3 problem. The talk will include material from: 1907.02534, 1908.03795, 2212.12565, appendix of 2511.04735.
