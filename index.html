Cosmology

Cosmological observables

Tomislav Prokopec · Utrecht University

Venue change — this talk only. We meet in the theatre in Wing III, not the Supek Theatre. The time is unchanged.

Most of us have gazed at the night sky, counted stars, and perhaps tried to identify familiar patterns and structures. In doing so, we have already taken the first step towards what modern observational cosmologists do: mapping the positions, shapes, sizes, and masses of distant galaxies and studying how they are distributed across the Universe. These measurements constitute cosmological observables.

A fundamental question is therefore: What physical processes gave rise to the galaxies and the large-scale structure of the Universe, and what determines their observed properties, including their distribution, shapes, and masses?

In this talk, I will discuss the leading theoretical framework for answering this question: the amplification of quantum vacuum fluctuations during cosmological inflation. According to this picture, microscopic quantum fluctuations in the early Universe were stretched to cosmological scales during inflation and subsequently evolved into the primordial perturbations from which galaxies and the large-scale structure eventually formed. We understand this mechanism remarkably well when interactions are neglected. The situation becomes much more involved once interactions with matter and gravity are included.

In this talk, I will present a program aimed at addressing this problem: developing a systematic framework for computing cosmological observables from interacting quantum cosmological perturbations, including their interactions with both matter and gravity. I will explain the conceptual and technical challenges involved and discuss the pitfalls that arise when attempting to make this connection between the fundamental quantum dynamics of the early Universe and the structures we observe in the sky.

About the Series

The Supek Physics Colloquium

The Supek Physics Colloquium is a seminar series of the Division of Theoretical Physics at the Ruđer Bošković Institute, named in honour of Ivan Supek (1915–2007), theoretical physicist, humanist, and one of the institute's founders. It brings together researchers from across theoretical physics: high-energy physics, solid-state physics, mathematical physics, cosmology and beyond, for talks aimed at a broad physics audience.

Talks take place on Wednesdays at 14:00 in the Lecture Theatre, Ivan Supek (1st) wing, with a shared Zoom link for remote attendees. Coffee is served afterward. Visitors from other institutions and students are warmly welcome.
